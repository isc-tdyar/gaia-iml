## Variable-star candidate triage

According to our analysis, among the sources flagged as variable by the challenge rule (relative flux swing above 100%), there is a significant portion that likely corresponds to genuine variable stars, although a considerable number are also instrumental artifacts, particularly in the slices with low sampling and high variability.

### What the slices show

The analysis of various slices of data indicates both consensus and discrepancies regarding the authenticity of variability:

- **Very Low SNR (under 50)**: This slice is complicated, especially with the significant ESA_reject_fraction of 0.7449 among sources with very few epochs. Here, variability is likely a mix of genuine and instrumental, reflecting the historical data's unreliability.

- **Low SNR (50-199)**: The mean ESA_reject_fraction of 0.1064 suggests some potential concerns with data quality, evidenced by the presence of near-zero minimum flux sources that could mislead variability metrics.

- **Good SNR (200-999)**: Despite having a high signal-to-noise ratio, the high ESA_reject_fraction of 0.1215 and individual sources with near-zero minimum flux suggest that reported variability might be inflated due to instrumental issues.

- **High SNR (1000+)**: While generally robust, one source's near-zero minimum flux raises similar concerns about potential overestimates of variability due to instrumental artifacts.

Overall, the slices indicate that while many flagged variables may be genuine, certain slices?especially those with low epochs and near-zero minimum flux?pose significant risks for contamination due to instrumentation.

### Likely artifacts

Estimates of probable instrumental contamination point towards substantial involvement, particularly in the slices with fewer epochs:

- For sources with very few epochs (under 5 in BP) in the **very low SNR** category, the ESA_reject_fraction averages around 0.7449, indicating over 74% of data points there may be noise-driven artifacts.
- The slice with **well sampled** conditions generally indicates higher reliability, with ESA_reject_fraction mean ranging around 0.0787, but even this reveals about 8% of their measurements may introduce instrumental biases.
- At the extreme end of variable swings (over 100,000%), there's clear evidence of contamination, with many sources showing near-zero minimum flux, suggesting that around 15.3% of reported variables are likely not astrophysical.

### Where the model is least reliable

Assessments of NGBoost_predicted_sigma and NGBoost_prediction_error reveal critical areas of uncertainty:

- In the **very low SNR** segment, the high NGBoost_predicted_sigma mean of 0.2204 indicates that this slice experiences significant model uncertainty, emphasizing caution in reliability assessments.
- Conversely, the **densely sampled** sources report a lower NGBoost_predicted_sigma mean of 0.0410, suggesting more robust estimates but still indicating caution due to the proximity of NGBoost_predicted_reject_fraction mean (0.1098), which approaches 0.1, hinting at uncertainty.
- The maximum NGBoost_predicted_sigma values across multiple slices?from **50-199** (max 0.3425) to **200-999** (max 0.0452)?illustrate a varying ability of the model to accurately characterize variability, suggesting the need for careful interpretation of results in slices characterized by high variability, low epochs, or near-zero flux.

In conclusion, our analysis cautions researchers to examine flags of variable sources closely, accounting for potential contaminants and model limitations while identifying plausible genuine variables within the datasets.
