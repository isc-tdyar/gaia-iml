## Survey data-quality audit

Across this survey, the photometry is most untrustworthy in the "heavy" and "severe" rejection slices. These populations exhibit the highest ESA reject fractions and substantial discrepancies in predicted versus observed rejections, indicating significant instrumental contamination.

### What the slices show

The analysis of the different slices reveals several trends and conflicts:

1. **Clean slice**: With a mean ESA_reject_fraction of 0.0286, this population shows a relatively low rejection rate and high variability. However, the high NGBoost_prediction_error of 0.0441 indicates that the predicted reject fraction possibly does not align well with ESA's measurements.

2. **Moderate slice**: The mean ESA_reject_fraction of 0.1075 is similar to the overall survey mean and suggests moderate reliability. The extreme flux swing observed here, while notable, is likely influenced by a few outliers. The NGBoost_predicted_sigma of 0.0441 further reinforces caution but is lower than in "heavy" and "severe" slices.

3. **Heavy slice**: The mean ESA_reject_fraction of 0.2647 suggests significant rejection due to data quality concerns. The high NGBoost_prediction_error of 0.0815 indicates major discrepancies in model predictions, hinting at possible instrumental effects.

4. **Severe slice**: The subcategories in this slice vary widely in properties. The "not variable" group shows high rejection rates (mean ESA_reject_fraction = 0.7736) with low variability, indicating likely instrumental artifacts. Conversely, both "variable, modest swing" (ESA_reject_fraction = 0.7030) and "variable, large swing" (ESA_reject_fraction = 0.6363) indicate high uncertainty and potentially significant artifacts, reiterating issues seen in the "heavy" slice. The "extreme swing" subgroup further shows pronounced discrepancies in reject fractions relative to the observed flux swings.

Overall, while variability is noted across most slices, there is consensus that higher rejection rates in the "heavy" and "severe" categories correlate with increased uncertainty and potential instrumental contamination, as evidenced by elevated NGBoost_prediction_error metrics.

### Likely artifacts

Quantifying probable instrumental contamination across the slices indicates a clear trend:

- In the **clean** slice, the ESA_reject_fraction of 0.0286 suggests minimal instrumental issues.
- The **moderate** slice, with a mean reject fraction of 0.1075 and high signal-to-noise ratios, presents some concern due to the notable presence of near-zero minimum flux sources.
- The **heavy** slice shows a mean ESA_reject_fraction of 0.2647, indicating substantial artifact presence.
- The **severe** slices are characterized by very high rejection rates, especially in the "not variable" (0.7736) and "variable, modest swing" (0.7030) subcategories, suggesting that data rejected in these groups likely suffer from substantial instrumental contamination.

Overall, higher ESA_reject_fraction levels suggest a stronger influence of artifacts in the more heavily rejected slices, with the most severe indicators in slices exhibiting near-zero minimal flux alongside high variability.

### Where the model is least reliable

The reliability of the model varies significantly across different slices, as indicated by the following:

- The overall survey statistics report an NGBoost_prediction_error of 0.0071 across the full dataset, indicating strong predictive performance on average.
- In the **clean** slice, the NGBoost_predicted_sigma of 0.0408 indicates relatively lower uncertainty in predictions.
- The **heavy** slice shows an increased NGBoost_predicted_sigma of 0.0626, denoting greater uncertainty as rejection rates increase.
- In the **severe** slice, particularly in "not variable" sources, the mean NGBoost_predicted_sigma of 0.2067 signals increased model uncertainty, which is compounded by the high NGBoost_prediction_error of 0.2471. Meanwhile, both the "variable, modest swing" and "variable, large swing" subcategories reflect increasingly high prediction errors (0.3135, 0.3107) and estimated sigma values (0.1422, 0.1059), suggesting considerable model reliability issues.

Ultimately, uncertainty in predictions escalates through the population from clean to severe, underlining a correlation between higher rejection rates and reduced model fidelity, which is most pronounced in slices with extreme variability or low epoch counts.

### How this report was produced

Generated by a recursive language model (RLM) pass over 74998 sources. The data was never placed in a prompt: each call received only aggregate statistics for its own slice, so context size is independent of table size. The root chose to decompose by `reject_level`; a slice was subdivided further when its own reject-fraction spread was still at least 1.0x the survey-wide spread of 0.0922 - i.e. when slicing had not made the population any more homogeneous - to a maximum depth of 3.

Recursion taken (10 LLM calls, ceiling 18):

```
  - clean (ESA rejected under 5% of epochs) (n=13208, sd=0.0157) -> summarized
  - moderate (5-20% rejected) (n=52301, sd=0.0380) -> summarized
  - heavy (20-50% rejected) (n=8844, sd=0.0600) -> summarized
  - severe (over half of all epochs rejected) (n=645, sd=0.1462) -> split by variability
    - not variable (swing under 100%) (n=150, sd=0.1554) -> summarized
    - variable, modest swing (100-1000%) (n=235, sd=0.1375) -> summarized
    - variable, large swing (1000-100000%) (n=193, sd=0.1219) -> summarized
    - extreme swing (over 100000%, almost certainly instrumental) (n=67, sd=0.1137) -> summarized
```
