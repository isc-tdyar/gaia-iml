## Survey data-quality audit (delegated)

The analysis indicates that the photometry is untrustworthy primarily in populations with very high rejection fractions and extreme variability, particularly those identified with severe rejection levels, low epoch counts, and concerns around signal quality. These populations correspond closely with those heavily flagged by ESA's pipeline and show mixed performance from our modeling efforts.

### What the slices show

The findings across different slices highlight several areas of agreement, particularly concerning the heavy rejection populations, where all slices report high ESA_reject_fraction values, indicating susceptibility to rejection due to extreme variance or instrumental issues. 

1. **`reject_level:heavy`** -  ESA_reject_fraction mean of 0.265 and NGBoost_predicted_reject_fraction mean of 0.183 indicate moderate and consistent rejection across the population.
2. **`reject_level:severe`** -  High ESA_reject_fraction of 0.6901 with substantial variability suggests intense data rejection due to strong variability. The model shows lower predictive power with a NGBoost_predicted_reject_fraction of 0.3951, accompanied by a high prediction error of 0.2950.
3. **`epoch_count:veryfew`** - Reflects high ESA_reject_fraction mean of 0.806, indicating that these sources are predominantly unreliable, with the model's NGBoost_predicted_reject_fraction showing good alignment at 0.726.
4. **`signal_quality:verylow`** - Presents a lower but considerable ESA_reject_fraction of 0.154, revealing variation between sources; however, many show the same trends of extreme variability.
5. **`model_confidence:veryunsure`** - Indicates high uncertainty with ESA_reject_fraction mean of 0.7515 and an NGBoost_prediction_error of 0.1741, suggesting mixed effectiveness in prediction within this chaotic population.
6. **`variability:extreme`** - Highlights mean ESA_reject_fraction of 0.1852 and various near-zero flux sources that complicate interpretations due to probable instrumental artifacts.

Overall, the slices largely agree about populations that face high ESA rejection, especially related to severe and very low epoch counts. Conflicting predictions from the NGBoost model and the ESA's pipeline highlight areas of uncertainty in establishing the reliability of contentious sources.

### Likely artifacts

We can quantify probable instrumental contamination as follows:

- **`reject_level:severe`**: 495 sources show flux swings over 100%, emphasizing substantial variability and likely artifacts.
- **`epoch_count:veryfew`**: Although indicating genuine variability, the scarcity of epochs (max of only 4) creates a high vulnerability to measurement artifacts.
- **`signal_quality:verylow`**: Over 95% of sources exhibit high flux swings over 100%, bolstered by the presence of sources with near-zero minimum flux, indicating potential instrumental contamination. 
- **`variability:extreme`**: 548 sources have near-zero flux, which can inflate perceived variability, further suggesting that extreme swings are likely driven by artifacts.

Overall, the slices suggest a recurring theme of high variability likely driven by instrumental issues, particularly notable in populations with high rejection rates.

### Where the model is least reliable

Regions flagged by the model as having high uncertainty include:

- **`reject_level:severe`**: With an NGBoost_prediction_error of 0.2950, the model struggles to predict the rejection fraction, revealing discrepancies between model expectations and ESA's assessments of data quality.
- **`model_confidence:veryunsure`**: This slice shows high prediction error at 0.1741 and high NGBoost_predicted_sigma of 0.2269, indicating uncertain model predictions in this diverse population.
- **`epoch_count:veryfew`**: Although the predicted reject fraction is notably high (0.726), the model's NGBoost_prediction_error of 0.0803 suggests there is still some level of concern regarding the quality of predictions, given the sparse data environment.

In conclusion, the model faces its most significant challenges in these highly variable and severely rejected populations, illustrating the difficulty in reliably assessing photometric data quality across such conditions.

### How this report was produced

Generated by a delegating recursive language model over 74998 sources. The data was never placed in a prompt: the only way to see it was a tool returning aggregate statistics for one slice, so context size is independent of table size.

Unlike `Gaia.RLM`, the decomposition here is the model's. It was shown the slice grammar and named the slices to analyze; each one was then handed to a `%AI.Agent.SubAgent` with the same tools, whose conclusion came back as one paragraph. Slice names are whitelist lookups rather than SQL, and the 6-delegation ceiling is enforced in ObjectScript, so neither depends on the model following instructions. The delegation budget was fully spent, so the exploration stopped where the trace ends rather than where the model chose to stop.
